# Market entry checklist

A market moves through four gates. Each gate has a short list of conditions; do not move to the next gate, or unlock the next budget tier, until every condition is green or consciously waived by the head of growth.

## Gate 0: Should we enter?

- [ ] Addressable market sized bottom-up (number of target businesses by size band, not a top-down percentage of GDP)
- [ ] Clear reason to win: at least one structural advantage versus the top two local alternatives
- [ ] Unit economics modelled with local prices, logistics costs and payment fees
- [ ] Regulatory, tax and invoicing requirements understood
- [ ] Executive sponsor and a named market lead
- [ ] Decision recorded with the assumptions that would make us reverse it

## Gate 1: Ready to launch

**Market intelligence**
- [ ] 10 to 15 customer interviews per primary segment
- [ ] Pain points documented by business size (small, mid, large)
- [ ] Competitive landscape: who, price position, channel presence, share of voice
- [ ] Seasonality calendar: peaks, troughs, holidays, industry events
- [ ] Buying journey mapped: who searches, who decides, who pays, how long it takes

**Positioning and messaging**
- [ ] Central pillars translated and adapted, reviewed by a native speaker from the industry
- [ ] Local proof points identified (or a plan to get them in the first 90 days)
- [ ] Message map per segment

**Infrastructure**
- [ ] Tracking: analytics, pixels, UTM convention, CRM fields mapped to the central model
- [ ] Landing pages localised (language, currency, units, trust signals, payment methods)
- [ ] Lead routing and SLA agreed with sales
- [ ] Market added to the central dashboard with the standard metric definitions

## Gate 2: Traction

Entered once launch is live. Exit when:

- [ ] At least two channels have produced customers at a CAC inside the launch band
- [ ] Conversion rates at each funnel stage are measured, not estimated
- [ ] First cohort retention data (at least 60 to 90 days)
- [ ] One local case study or testimonial published
- [ ] The country playbook has been revised with real data at least once

## Gate 3: Ready to scale

- [ ] CAC payback inside the target for two consecutive months
- [ ] Primary channel shows no sharp rise in marginal CAC at the current spend level
- [ ] Sales and operations capacity can absorb two times the current volume
- [ ] Retention for the oldest cohort is at or above the regional benchmark
- [ ] A test backlog of at least ten prioritised ideas for the market

## Exit criteria (stop or pause)

Agree these before launch, not after:

- CAC above two times the launch band for three months with no improving trend
- Fewer than N customers after M months (set N and M at gate 0)
- A regulatory or operational blocker with no resolution date
