# Central vs local responsibilities

The single most important design decision in a multi-market growth team is the line between what the centre decides and what each market decides. Draw it explicitly, write it down, and revisit it every quarter.

## Principle

> The centre owns anything that must be **comparable or consistent** across markets. The market owns anything that must be **true to local reality**.

If a decision changes how a metric is calculated, how the brand is described or how budget is allocated, it is central. If it depends on who the buyer is, when they buy, or what they compare you to, it is local.

## Responsibility map

| Area | Central growth strategy (regional) | Local market intelligence (country) |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning | Brand pillars, value proposition, tone of voice, what we never say | Proof points, local language and idioms, competitor framing |
| Audience | ICP definition and segmentation logic (e.g. by business size) | Deep audience research, local sub-segments, pain points by size |
| Channels | Channel toolkit, platform accounts, naming conventions, tracking standards | Channel mix and weighting, local partners, events calendar |
| Paid media | Efficiency rules (target CAC bands, bidding guardrails, creative testing framework) | Campaign execution, local creative adaptation, agency management |
| CRM | Lifecycle architecture, segmentation fields, consent rules | Message content, send timing, local offers |
| Sales enablement | Core pitch deck, objection library, lead routing rules | Local case studies, pricing explanations, field feedback loop |
| Seasonality | Regional calendar template | Local consumption patterns and seasonal peaks |
| Measurement | Metric definitions, attribution model, dashboards, reporting cadence | Data quality in the market, context and commentary on the numbers |
| Budget | Total envelope, allocation by stage, monthly reallocation | Spend within the envelope, requests for more with evidence |
| Experiments | Test library, prioritisation method, cross-market rollout | Running tests, proposing local hypotheses |

## RACI for the decisions that cause the most friction

R = responsible, A = accountable, C = consulted, I = informed.

| Decision | Head of growth (central) | Market lead | Performance / paid | CRM | Sales | Product |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Change a positioning pillar | A | C | I | I | C | C |
| Adapt messaging for a market | C | A/R | C | C | C | I |
| Add a new channel in a market | A | R | C | I | I | I |
| Move budget between markets | A/R | C | C | I | I | I |
| Change a metric definition | A/R | C | C | C | C | C |
| Launch a local test | I | A/R | R | R | C | C |
| Roll out a winning test to other markets | A | R (each) | R | R | I | C |
| Request product change for a market | C | R | I | I | C | A |

## Operating rhythm

| Cadence | Forum | Owner | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Market review (30 min) | Head of growth | Actions per market, test decisions |
| Monthly | Budget reallocation | Head of growth + finance | New envelope per market and channel |
| Quarterly | Playbook review | Each market lead | Updated country playbook, stage reassessment |
| Quarterly | Positioning check | Head of growth + product | Confirmed or revised pillars |

## Warning signs that the line is in the wrong place

- Markets report CAC with different definitions. The measurement line is too loose.
- Every local campaign needs central sign-off. The execution line is too tight, and speed will suffer.
- A winning test in one market is never tried elsewhere. Nobody owns cross-market rollout.
- Budget per market has not changed in three months. Allocation is habit, not evidence.
- Local teams describe the product differently from the website. Positioning is not shared.
